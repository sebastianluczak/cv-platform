== 1. Overview

The *Market Demand Observatory* is a capability of CV-Platform that analyzes job advertisements over time to identify trends in technology demand, skill combinations, and role requirements.

The existing platform understands individual job advertisements. The Observatory aggregates those advertisements to understand how the observed job market changes over time.

=== 1.1. Objectives

* Identify technologies and skills that appear most frequently in job advertisements.
* Detect skills whose prevalence is increasing or decreasing.
* Discover combinations of skills that frequently appear together.
* Identify skills characteristic of specific role categories.
* Provide evidence-backed market insights based on collected advertisements.
* Enable future integration with candidate-facing features such as Career Pilot and Adaptive Interview Simulator.

=== 1.2. MVP scope

The initial release shall:

. Collect job advertisements from a small number of permitted sources.
. Normalize and deduplicate collected advertisements.
. Reuse existing CV-Platform extraction and skill-matching functionality.
. Persist normalized advertisements and historical observations.
. Calculate daily market statistics.
. Expose statistics through an API.
. Present trends and supporting evidence in a React dashboard.

=== 1.3. Non-goals

The initial release will not attempt to:

* Predict future job-market conditions.
* Collect advertisements from every available job board.
* Automatically apply for jobs.
* Provide salary predictions.
* Use an LLM as the primary source of market conclusions.
* Introduce a dedicated analytics database before demonstrated need.

== 2. Business Reports

=== 2.1. Skill Popularity

Identify technologies and skills most frequently mentioned in eligible job advertisements.

Example:

[quote]
____
Go appears in 18% of observed backend engineering advertisements in Poland during September.
____

Reports must include the observation period, sample size, market definition, and contributing sources.

=== 2.2. Skill Momentum

Compare skill prevalence across two periods of equal length.

Example:

[quote]
____
Go prevalence increased from 12% to 18% between consecutive observation periods, a change of 6 percentage points.
____

Display percentage-point changes separately from relative percentage growth.

=== 2.3. Skill Combinations

Identify technologies and skills that frequently appear together.

Example combinations:

* Go + distributed systems
* Go + AI infrastructure
* Kubernetes + platform engineering
* Rust + embedded systems

The system should distinguish frequently occurring combinations from combinations that are unusually associated with one another.

=== 2.4. Role Fingerprints

Calculate skill prevalence separately for role categories such as:

* Backend Engineer
* Platform Engineer
* DevOps Engineer
* Software Architect
* Data Engineer
* Machine Learning Engineer

Identify skills that distinguish one role category from another.

Role categories must be normalized and consistently applied across the historical dataset.

== 3. Architecture

=== 3.1. High-Level Processing Flow

[source,text]
----
Job Sources
    |
    v
Collectors / Source Adapters
    |
    v
Raw Job Archive (Object Storage)
    |
    v
Existing Processing Pipeline
    |
    +--> Extraction
    +--> Normalization
    +--> Skill Matching
    +--> Role Classification
    |
    v
Historical Job Store
    |
    v
Market Analytics / Aggregation
    |
    v
REST API
    |
    v
React Dashboard
----

=== 3.2. Job Sources

Potential source types include:

* Public job APIs.
* Sources explicitly permitting automated collection.
* Permitted feeds and exports.
* Employer career pages where automated access is allowed.

Source selection must consider availability, coverage, data quality, rate limits, terms of service, and retention requirements.

=== 3.3. Collectors

Collectors are responsible for retrieving advertisements and converting source-specific responses into a normalized representation.

Collectors must not independently implement skill extraction or market analytics.

Responsibilities:

* Source-specific fetching and pagination.
* Incremental collection.
* Normalization of source fields.
* Source record identification.
* Collection timestamps and provenance.
* Rate limiting and retry handling.
* Reporting collection errors.
* Detecting source-level changes and disappearance.

Each source adapter should be independently testable.

=== 3.4. Existing Processing Pipeline

Reuse the existing CV-Platform services wherever practical:

* `extractor` — extract structured information from source documents.
* `skillmatcher` — identify normalized skills.
* `offermatcher` — reuse applicable job and offer matching logic.
* `aiweighter` — reuse only where its weighting functionality is relevant.
* `common` — reuse existing messaging, object storage, and observability integrations.

Existing services should remain the authoritative implementation of their respective capabilities.

=== 3.5. New Logical Components

==== `jobcollector`

Responsibilities:

* Source adapters.
* Collection scheduling or invocation.
* Incremental fetching.
* Raw advertisement normalization.
* Collection metadata and error reporting.

==== `marketinsights`

Responsibilities:

* Historical aggregation.
* Skill prevalence calculations.
* Skill co-occurrence calculations.
* Role-specific statistics.
* Period comparisons.
* Market analytics API.

These components may initially be packages or standalone Go binaries. Separate deployment should be justified by independent scaling, reliability, or operational requirements.

== 4. Persistence Model

=== 4.1. Database Selection

MongoDB is a reasonable initial choice because advertisements are semi-structured documents and source-specific fields may vary.

PostgreSQL is also suitable, particularly if relational queries, constraints, and SQL-based analytics become important.

The initial implementation should use the database that best fits the existing operational environment.

A dedicated time-series database is not required for the MVP.

=== 4.3. `job_observations`

Stores dated observations of advertisements.

Suggested fields:

[cols="1,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Field |Type |Purpose

|`offer_id`
|Identifier
|Reference to the canonical offer.

|`source_id`
|String
|Source that was queried.

|`observed_at`
|Timestamp
|Time the advertisement was observed.

An observation records what the collector actually saw. It must not be interpreted as proof that an advertisement was originally published on that date.

Repeated identical observations may be coalesced according to a documented collection policy, provided meaningful lifecycle and content changes remain recoverable.

=== 4.5. `market_skill_pairs`

Optional collection for precomputed skill co-occurrence statistics.

Suggested fields:

* `date`
* `market_key`
* `skill_a`
* `skill_b`
* `offer_count`
* `pair_count`
* `pair_share`

Store each pair in a canonical order so that `(Go, Kubernetes)` and `(Kubernetes, Go)` cannot create duplicate records.

=== 4.6. Raw Object Storage

Large raw advertisements and source snapshots may be stored in object storage.

Database records should retain:

* Object reference.
* Content hash.
* Source identifier.
* Collection timestamp.
* Processing version.

Raw content retention must comply with source permissions and applicable data-retention requirements.

=== 4.7. Data Integrity Requirements

* Use stable identifiers for normalized skills.
* Enforce unique constraints for canonical records and logical observation keys.
* Make collection retries idempotent.
* Keep advertisements separate from their historical observations.
* Preserve provenance and processing versions.
* Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.
* Support consistent historical reporting.

== 5. Statistical Engine

=== 5.1. Skill Prevalence

For a skill `s` and observation period `t`:

[source,text]
----
prevalence(s, t) =
    distinct eligible offers mentioning s
    --------------------------------------- * 100
         distinct eligible offers
----

Example:

* Eligible advertisements: 1,000.
* Advertisements mentioning Go: 180.
* Go prevalence: 18%.

The denominator must be explicitly defined and consistently applied.

=== 5.2. Skill Momentum

Compare prevalence between two periods of equal length.

[source,text]
----
absolute_change =
    recent_prevalence - previous_prevalence
----

Example:

* Previous prevalence: 12%.
* Recent prevalence: 18%.
* Change: +6 percentage points.
* Relative growth: +50%.

Both measures may be reported, but percentage-point change should be the primary presentation.

=== 5.3. Skill Co-occurrence

For skills `A` and `B`:

[source,text]
----
pair_share =
    eligible offers mentioning both A and B
    --------------------------------------- * 100
             distinct eligible offers
----

This metric measures how often the combination appears in the observed market.

=== 5.4. Skill Association

Pair share alone does not establish that two skills are unusually associated.

A useful additional metric is lift:

[source,text]
----
lift(A, B) = P(A and B) / (P(A) * P(B))
----

Interpretation:

* Lift greater than 1: the pair occurs more frequently than the independence baseline.
* Lift approximately equal to 1: occurrence is consistent with independence.
* Lift less than 1: the pair occurs less frequently than the independence baseline.

Lift should be interpreted alongside sample size and the individual prevalence of each skill.

=== 5.5. Role Fingerprints

Calculate skill prevalence independently for each role category.

Possible outputs:

* Most common skills in a role.
* Skills with unusually high prevalence relative to other roles.
* Skill combinations associated with a role.
* Changes in role requirements over time.

Role classifications and skill mappings must remain consistent across comparison periods.

=== 5.6. Source Coverage

Every report must provide enough context to interpret its statistics.

At minimum, report:

* Observation period.
* Number of distinct eligible advertisements.
* Number of advertisements matching the selected skill or combination.
* Contributing sources.
* Source coverage changes.
* Relevant methodology or processing-version changes.

A change in source composition can create an apparent trend without a genuine change in the underlying market.

=== 5.7. Interpretation Boundaries

The Observatory measures advertisements collected under a defined collection strategy. It does not automatically measure the entire labor market.

Reports must distinguish observed prevalence from claims about total market demand.

Forecasting, causal explanations, and claims about future hiring demand are outside the initial statistical scope.

== 6. Collector Requirements

=== 6.1. Source Adapter Interface

A preliminary Go interface could look like this:

[source,go]
----
type JobSource interface {
    Name() string
    Fetch(ctx context.Context, cursor string) (FetchResult, error)
}

type FetchResult struct {
    Offers     []RawOffer
    NextCursor string
    HasMore    bool
}

type RawOffer struct {
    ExternalID  string
    URL         string
    Title       string
    Company     string
    Location    string
    Description string
    PublishedAt *time.Time
}
----

This is an initial sketch rather than a finalized interface. The production contract should account for source-specific identifiers, pagination, metadata, and lifecycle behavior.

=== 6.2. Collection Requirements

Collectors must support:

* Source adapters with independently configurable limits.
* Incremental fetching and pagination.
* Idempotent retries.
* Request timeouts and bounded retries.
* Exponential backoff where appropriate.
* Rate limiting and concurrency limits.
* Structured logs and operational metrics.
* Collection run identifiers.
* Source provenance and timestamps.
* Partial failure isolation.
* Detection of content changes.
* Monitoring of successful and unsuccessful collection runs.

=== 6.3. Lifecycle Detection

An advertisement disappearing from a source must not immediately be interpreted as closed.

The collector should distinguish:

* Successfully observed.
* Not observed during a successful subsequent scan.
* Confirmed unavailable.
* Source collection failed.
* Reappeared after a period of absence.

Lifecycle transitions should be recorded with their evidence and observation times.

=== 6.4. Source Compliance

* Prefer permitted APIs and feeds.
* Respect source terms and applicable access restrictions.
* Apply reasonable rate limits.
* Follow relevant crawling policies.
* Respect retention and redistribution constraints.
* Avoid unnecessary personal-data collection.

The initial release should prioritize one or two reliable, permitted sources over broad but unreliable coverage.

== 7. Dashboard Requirements

=== 7.1. Market Overview

Display:

* Number of distinct observed advertisements.
* Selected market and role filters.
* Selected observation period.
* Skill prevalence.
* Period-over-period change.
* Source coverage.
* Data freshness.

=== 7.2. Skill Demand Over Time

Display time-series charts for selected skills.

Users should be able to:

* Select skills.
* Change the observation period.
* Filter by market and role.
* Compare prevalence.
* Inspect sample sizes.
* View source coverage changes.

=== 7.3. Emerging Skill Combinations

Display combinations with notable prevalence or changes in prevalence.

Each combination should include:

* Skill identifiers and display names.
* Pair share.
* Period-over-period change.
* Number of supporting advertisements.
* Relevant role categories.
* Source coverage.
* Links to supporting advertisements, subject to applicable access and privacy policies.

=== 7.4. Evidence Inspection

Users should be able to inspect the advertisements supporting a statistic.

The evidence view should explain:

* Which advertisements contributed to the numerator.
* How the denominator was calculated.
* Which filters were applied.
* Which source supplied each advertisement.
* Which extraction and matching versions were used.

=== 7.5. Data Integrity Indicators

Display warnings when:

* Sample sizes are small.
* Source coverage changes substantially.
* A source has not been collected recently.
* Historical records have been reprocessed.
* Skill-matching methodology changes affect comparability.

All example statistics shown during development must be clearly marked as illustrative rather than actual market observations.

== 8. API Requirements

The initial REST API should support:

* Retrieving available markets and role categories.
* Listing normalized skills.
* Querying skill prevalence over a time range.
* Comparing skill prevalence across periods.
* Querying skill combinations.
* Retrieving role fingerprints.
* Retrieving source coverage and data freshness.
* Inspecting supporting advertisements where permitted.

API responses should include the selected market, observation period, denominator, matching count, and relevant methodology metadata.

Aggregation should be performed in the analytics layer rather than independently in the React client.

== 9. Processing Versions and Historical Consistency

Skill extraction and normalization may improve over time. These changes can alter historical statistics even when the underlying advertisements have not changed.

The system must record relevant processing versions, including:

* Extractor version.
* Skill matcher version.
* Skill dictionary or ontology version.
* Role classifier version.
* Aggregation methodology version.

When processing logic changes, historical data should either be reprocessed consistently or clearly marked as methodologically different.

The system must not silently interpret increased extraction coverage as increased market demand.

Historical reports should be reproducible from retained source observations and documented processing rules, subject to the available data and retention constraints.

== 10. Implementation Plan

=== Milestone 1: Collection and Normalization

. Implement one permitted source adapter.
. Normalize the advertisement fields.
. Persist canonical offers and dated observations.
. Add deduplication and idempotency constraints.
. Preserve source provenance and content hashes.
. Add collection metrics and structured error reporting.

*Exit criteria:* Repeated collection runs do not create duplicate records, and collection failures are distinguishable from advertisement disappearance.

=== Milestone 2: Existing Pipeline Integration

. Send normalized advertisements through the existing processing pipeline.
. Reuse the existing extractor and skill matcher.
. Persist stable skill identifiers and role classifications.
. Record processing versions.
. Support reprocessing when extraction or matching logic changes.

*Exit criteria:* Collected advertisements have normalized skills, traceable provenance, and identifiable processing versions.

=== Milestone 3: Daily Statistics

. Calculate skill prevalence.
. Calculate skill co-occurrence.
. Calculate period-over-period changes.
. Generate role fingerprints.
. Track source coverage.
. Store precomputed daily statistics.
. Add tests for denominators, deduplication, and historical consistency.

*Exit criteria:* Reports can be reproduced and interpreted using counts, denominators, time windows, and source coverage.

=== Milestone 4: API and Dashboard

. Implement the analytics REST API.
. Add the React market overview.
. Add skill trend charts.
. Add skill combination reports.
. Add role filters and date ranges.
. Add evidence inspection.
. Display data freshness and sample sizes.

*Exit criteria:* A user can explore a trend and inspect the advertisements and calculations supporting it.

== 11. Acceptance Criteria

The MVP is ready when:

. Advertisements can be collected repeatedly without duplicate canonical records.
. Failed collection runs do not incorrectly mark all advertisements as unavailable.
. Historical observations remain distinguishable from publication dates.
. Advertisements can be reprocessed after extraction or matching improvements.
. Skill prevalence can be calculated for a selected market and observation period.
. Skill co-occurrence can be calculated for a selected pair of skills.
. Period-over-period changes are reproducible.
. Every report exposes its numerator, denominator, sample size, and contributing sources.
. Changes in source coverage are visible to users.
. Changes in processing methodology are recorded.
. Users can inspect the evidence behind a reported trend.
. Example data is clearly distinguished from real collected statistics.

== 13. Future Extensions

The Observatory can eventually support:

* Salary and compensation intelligence.
* Skill-demand forecasting.
* Geographic and industry comparisons.
* Seniority-specific market analysis.
* Candidate-specific opportunity recommendations.
* Personalized skill-gap analysis.
* Adaptive Interview Simulator question generation.
* Career Pilot opportunity discovery.
* API access for third-party career platforms.
* Historical analysis of how job requirements evolve.

These extensions should build on reliable historical observations and transparent statistics rather than replacing them.

== 14. Guiding Principle

[quote]
____
Optimize for trustworthy market intelligence, not the volume of advertisements collected or the number of AI-generated insights produced.
____

The Observatory should help users understand what the collected evidence supports, what has changed, how reliable the conclusion is, and where uncertainty remains.
